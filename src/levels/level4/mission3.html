      Why it works

      Every automation is a trigger (something happens) followed by actions (do this, then that). Filters sit in between and decide whether the chain keeps going. The AI step is just one link.

      Most bad days come from three things: the same entry arriving twice, an entry with missing or broken data, and a message leaving before anyone read it. A filter, a repeat check, and a draft step cover all three.

      Switching on a new automation safely

      Test with made-up entries first, including a messy one and a double. Start in draft mode and read every output for a couple of weeks. Only consider auto-send for the kinds of messages that have stayed clean the whole time.

      Keep one visible off switch, a daily limit, and a log you can read. Any tool you use will have its own names for these. The ideas are the same everywhere.
