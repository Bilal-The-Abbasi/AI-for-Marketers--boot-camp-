Why it works

An AI writing tool reads everything so far and rates every possible next word by how likely it is. Then it picks one, adds it, and repeats. That is the whole trick, run thousands of times per answer.

It is not looking up facts or deciding what is true. It is ranking what usually follows what. That is why it writes smoothly even when it is wrong, and why context matters so much. Change the voice in the first lines and the whole ranking shifts.

Tokens, temperature, and why you get different answers

The model works in tokens, which are chunks of words rather than whole words. After rating the options it does not always take the top one. A setting called temperature controls how adventurous the pick is.

Low temperature plays it safe and repeats itself. High temperature takes more risks. That is why the same prompt can return a different answer each time you run it. Most chat tools set this for you.

    Why it works

    Remember the odds from Mission 1? Every line you add narrows the field. With no audience, the AI guesses “everyone” and writes for no one. Give it an audience, a goal, some rules, and a voice, and it stops guessing at things you already know.

    Better prompts are mostly better briefs, the same kind you would hand a freelancer on day one. Voice is the hardest to put in words. “Friendly” means almost nothing. One real subject line that worked shows it in a single line.

    Fix a near miss without starting over

    If the output is close but off, do not start a new chat. Tell it what to change: “shorter, drier, drop the second line.” It keeps the context and adjusts, and you spend your effort on the part that is actually wrong.

    When you can, show instead of describe. One example of the result you want usually beats a paragraph of adjectives.

    Why it works

    It ranks likely words. It does not check facts. A statistic with a source attached sounds right because real statistics with sources are common in what it learned from, so it produces one with the right shape and nothing behind it.

    Four tells give most of them away. Run this 10-second check on anything you plan to publish.

    • NumbersDid I give it this number?
    • Quotes and namesDid this person really say this?
    • Dates and “latest”Could this have changed since it learned it?
    • Always and neverIs it really that absolute?

    How to make it more honest

    Paste in your own facts and tell it to use only those. Tell it to ask you, or say “I don’t know,” when something is missing. If your tool has web search, turn it on, then open the sources it cites, because it can still cite the wrong thing.

    None of this removes the check. It just makes the pile of things to check smaller.
