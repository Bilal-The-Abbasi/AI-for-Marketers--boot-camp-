    Where AI research is useful, and where it is not

    It is good at structure: turning your messy notes into themes, suggesting questions to ask customers, and spotting gaps in what you have. It is weak at facts about the outside world, especially specific numbers and named sources.

    Use it to get oriented fast. Then take every claim you plan to act on to a source you can open, or to your own data.

    Why it works

    The pattern: AI is fast at the first pass, and you own the parts that depend on your readers, your facts, and your name. Intent and fact checking are the two steps people skip, and they are the two that decide whether a post helps anyone.

    Good search content has not changed much. Match what the searcher came for, say something only you can say, get it right, and make the next step easy.

    Check

    Go deeper

    A prompt for each step

    Intent: “Here is the keyword and three questions our customers ask. List what someone searching this wants to know and do next.” Outline: “Using that, propose an outline. Mark any section where only we have something to add.”

    Draft: “Write from my notes only. Leave a gap marked [NEED FACT] wherever you would have to guess.” Fact check: “List every claim in this draft that I should verify.” Then you do the verifying.

    Why it works

    Clicks are partly skill and partly luck. With small numbers, luck is loud: a few clicks either way can flip the winner. That is why the same ad beat itself. More people smooths the luck out. Changing one thing means a real gap points at something.

    AI is great for the variants. Ask for them like this, then test two or three, not twenty.

    What “wait” really means

    Decide the end date and the audience before you start, then do not peek and stop early because one side is ahead. Early leads often fade.

    Also run both versions at the same time. Different days and different weeks bring different shoppers, and that muddies the result.

    Why it works

    The good question asks what moved and what else could explain it, and keeps the AI inside your table. The shaky claims share a pattern: a cause read from timing, a conclusion from a handful of orders, and a start and end date picked to look good.

    AI does the arithmetic and the wording well. You still own the question, and the check.
